Current projects

Dynamic material flow analysis of global data center hardware and infrastructure

With S. Mahjoubi and E. Olivetti · Draft in progress · Presented at ISIE-SEM 2026

I quantify embedded material stocks and flows across data center subsystems: IT hardware, power and cooling equipment, and the building shell, and model critical and bulk material trajectories under capacity growth scenarios. Weibull-based lifetime distributions capture how differences in subsystem lifetimes shape long-term material demand and end-of-life outflows, and Monte Carlo simulation propagates uncertainty through material intensities and growth assumptions. The model also explores recovery scenarios for critical minerals at end of life.

Critical material supply risk exposure across semiconductor technology nodes

With A. Gupta, G. Peters, E. Olivetti, and G. Norris · Draft in progress

I assess how vulnerable semiconductor manufacturing is to critical mineral supply risk, and how that vulnerability changes across fabrication processes and technology nodes. The work adapts an exposure-based supply risk framework from automotive electrification research, combining bottom-up material use in fabs with top-down indicators of supply concentration and import reliance.

Discernible carbon savings in server refresh decisions

With N. Bashir and E. Olivetti · Draft in progress · Thesis chapter: “The Hidden Cost of Upgrades”

When does replacing a server with a newer, more efficient one actually reduce emissions? This project couples three pieces: a streamlined LCA model of server embodied carbon with Monte Carlo uncertainty, empirical power measurements across 12 enterprise servers on the Chameleon Cloud testbed, and a system dynamics model of fleet replacement. I use discernibility statistics to test whether carbon differences between servers hold up under uncertainty, and find that an intermediate server lifetime (neither aggressive refresh nor long retention) minimizes cumulative emissions.

Uncertainty-aware carbon footprint modeling for sustainable computing

Led by N. Bashir, with the Olivetti and Delimitrou groups and industry collaborators · Draft in progress

This collaboration develops uncertainty-aware carbon footprint models for server hardware and semiconductor-intensive systems. By quantifying data gaps and performing sensitivity analysis, we identify which parameters and supply-chain assumptions matter most for sustainable computing decisions.

PAIA – Product Attributes to Impact Algorithm

With A. Gupta, R. Kirchain, G. Norris, and E. Olivetti · Industry-facing methodology

I contribute to PAIA, a streamlined LCA methodology that links product-level attributes of ICT equipment to environmental impacts across Scope 1–3 emissions, helping manufacturers estimate footprints when full inventory data are not available.

Dependence-aware uncertainty and sensitivity analysis for LCA

Methods project · In development

Most LCA uncertainty analyses still sample inputs independently, which can break mass balances and distort comparisons between products. I am developing a statistical framework for uncertainty propagation and global sensitivity analysis that respects how LCA inputs actually depend on each other: correlated and non-Gaussian inputs (e.g., copulas), compositional and mass-balance constraints (e.g., Dirichlet distributions), and joint sensitivity across multiple impact categories. The aim is a method that preserves physical structure, scales to full LCA models, and tells practitioners when a comparative conclusion is robust.

Cooling energy, cost, and PUE risk across US data centers

Modeling project · In development

How exposed are data center operating costs to climate? I am modeling how cooling energy responds to outdoor temperature across US climate zones, and how that propagates to power usage effectiveness (PUE) and cost. The core chain is simple: cooling energy as a function of temperature, and cost as energy multiplied by the local electricity price, but combining it with climate variability, warming scenarios, and regional price differences turns it into a risk model for siting and operating decisions.

Quantifying the coupled material and water trade-offs of the data center cooling transition

In development

Rising rack power densities are pushing data centers from air cooling toward rear-door heat exchangers, direct-to-chip liquid cooling, and immersion. Liquid cooling allows heat to be rejected at higher temperatures, which can make dry heat rejection feasible and cut on-site water use. But dry coolers need far more heat transfer surface, and therefore more copper, aluminum, and steel. I am building a parametric material intensity model across six cooling architectures and five heat rejection equipment classes, drawing on manufacturer submittal data, Open Compute Project specifications, environmental product declarations, and first-principles thermal sizing, with Monte Carlo uncertainty and global sensitivity analysis. Applied across US climate zones and water stress categories, the model maps the water–material trade-off frontier and projects material demand, in-use stock, and end-of-life availability to 2040.

Spatially explicit material stocks and end-of-life flows across US data centers

In development · Builds on the 1.C51 team project below

National and global material flow analyses hide where data center materials actually accumulate. This project builds a facility-resolved dynamic MFA of the US data center fleet, estimating in-place stocks of copper, aluminum, steel, and selected critical minerals, and projecting end-of-life outflows with layer-specific lifetimes for IT equipment, mechanical and electrical systems, and building structure. Facility-level results are intersected with water stress (WRI Aqueduct 4.0), grid carbon intensity (EPA eGRID), and community environmental burden (CDC Environmental Justice Index) to ask where material is accumulating, whether recovery capacity exists where retirement waves will arrive, and where those waves coincide with regions already under environmental stress.
